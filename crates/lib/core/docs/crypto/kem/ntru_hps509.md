NTRU-HPS-2048-509 encapsulation with Eidos hashing.<br /><br />Encapsulation uses the recipient's public key and fresh randomness to produce a shared secret<br />and a capsule, the KEM ciphertext:<br /><br />`Encapsulate(recipient_public_key, fresh_randomness) -> (capsule, shared_secret)`<br />`Decapsulate(recipient_private_key, capsule) -> shared_secret`<br /><br />The sender sends the capsule to the recipient, who uses its private key to recover the same<br />secret. That secret supplies key material for encrypting the application message; the capsule<br />is separate from the encrypted message. Use `crypto::ies::ntru_eidos::seal` to combine these<br />operations.<br /><br />Let h be the recipient's public polynomial and r, m be sampled polynomials with trits 0, 1, 2.<br />The lift maps 0, 1, 2 to 0, 1, 2047, representing 0, 1, -1 modulo 2048. Encapsulation computes<br />c = h * lift(r) + lift(m) modulo (X^509 - 1, 2048), then hashes the encodings of r and m<br />to obtain the shared secret. Here m is a sampled secret, not the application message;<br />it has 127 trits of each nonzero value.<br /><br />The host supplies h, r, m, and the integer product P = h * lift(r) through advice.<br />Instead of multiplying the polynomials in the VM, this module checks P at two extension-field<br />points derived from a hash of h, P, r, and m. Two points meet the polynomial check's 128-bit<br />soundness target in the ideal-hash model.<br />The module also checks h against the public-key commitment, then checks the trit encodings and<br />m's weight before constructing the capsule.<br />
