
## miden::core::stark::security
| Procedure | Description |
| ----------- | ------------- |
| compute_conjectured_security_level | Computes a proof's conjectured security level from the descriptor returned by the MVM or PVM<br />recursive verifier:<br /><br />level = min(query, lookup).<br /><br />The native report also includes constraint composition, out-of-domain evaluation, DEEP<br />composition, FRI folding, and the field/hash cap. The bounds checked here ensure that these<br />five terms are greater than lookup, as shown below. Lifted STARK applies grinding before DEEP,<br />FRI folding, and query sampling, but not before lookup challenges; lookup grinding must be zero.<br /><br /># Query term<br /><br />query = floor(num_queries * BITS_PER_QUERY_FP / 2^16) + query_pow_bits.<br /><br />This is the native fixed-point query calculation, using the full extension-field cardinality.<br /><br /># Lookup term<br /><br />Let A = (max_message_width + 2) * fractions_per_row, q = ceil(log2(A)), and<br />R = r_w * FP_ONE + r_f be the Q16 correction for one-time boundary fractions. Define<br /><br />s = q * FP_ONE - ceil(FP_ONE * log2(A)).<br /><br />Since the sampling support has exactly 126 bits, the whole-bit lookup bound is<br /><br />lookup = 126 - q - height - r_w - borrow,<br />borrow = 1 if s < r_f, otherwise 0.<br /><br />To avoid a fixed-point logarithm, set P = 2^q and g = P - A. The positive power series<br />-log2(1 - x) = log2(e) * (x + x^2/2 + ...) gives the integer lower bound<br /><br />t1 = floor(LOG2_E_FP * g / P),<br />b = t1 + floor(t1 * g / (2 * P)) <= s.<br /><br />The procedure subtracts one when b < r_f. Because b <= s, it cannot overstate the native<br />bound and can understate it by at most one bit. For MVM, A = (16 + 2) * 78 = 1404 gives<br />b = 34404, while R is at most 4887 (258 boundary terms at height 6). For PVM,<br />A = (18 + 2) * 260 = 5200 gives b = 40838 and R = 1 at every supported height. Hence<br />b >= r_f for both deployed shapes, so this decision is exact throughout their ranges.<br /><br />The accepted coefficient range is 257..=65536. Each factor is also limited to 65536 so their<br />product cannot wrap. At widths 16 and 18, the upper bound permits 3640 and 3276 fractions per<br />row respectively. The descriptor supplies these values from the verified AIR shape.<br /><br /># Why the other five terms can be omitted<br /><br />A >= 257, height >= 6, and R >= 0 give<br /><br />lookup <= floor(126 - log2(257) - height) = 117 - height <= 111.<br /><br />Each omitted term has a larger lower bound:<br /><br />- out-of-domain: log_blowup = 3 permits at most eight quotient chunks, and both verifiers reject<br />AIRs of degree greater than nine. With local and next openings, the DEEP-ALI identity degree<br />is at most max(9 * (H + 1) + H - 1, 9 * H + 1) <= 11 * H for H = 2^height >= 64.<br />OOD sampling rejects base-field points, costing one Q16 unit in the native bound. Since<br />ceil(FP_ONE * log2(11)) + 1 < 4 * FP_ONE, the term still exceeds 122 - height;<br />- FRI folding: FRI_FOLDING_BASE_BITS - height + folding_pow >= 120 - height;<br />- composition: composed <= 8192 gives at least 126 - log2(8192) = 113 bits;<br />- DEEP: deep_terms <= 8192 and nonnegative grinding also give at least 113 bits;<br />- collision cap: SECURITY_CAP_BITS = 126 > 111.<br /><br />The OOD and folding bounds subtract the same height as lookup. Thus their margins hold at<br />every accepted height. DEEP and folding grinding can only increase their bounds. The cap is<br />also greater than lookup, so omitting a query clamp cannot affect the final minimum.<br /><br /># Contract<br /><br />Every descriptor field is checked before it is used or discarded. The lower bounds on height<br />and the lookup coefficient support the proof that the other terms cannot determine the result.<br />The upper bounds on height, coefficient, and boundary terms keep the arithmetic in range, and<br />the explicit `base >= 1` check validates the lookup subtraction after applying the correction.<br />Requiring zero lookup grinding keeps the native cap irrelevant to the lookup term. An<br />unsupported input traps instead of returning a security level.<br /><br />This is not a general-purpose estimator for caller-assembled parameters. Its input must be the<br />descriptor returned by a successful MVM or PVM recursive verifier, without modification. The<br />procedure is loop-free and has bounded execution cost for every accepted descriptor.<br /><br />The meaning of `num_lookup_boundary_terms` is the same for both relations even though its source<br />differs. The MVM adds the kernel-procedure count to three fixed core-boundary terms. The PVM<br />uses the number of fixed `UintVal` and `EcGroup` messages added at its verifier boundary. The<br />estimator needs only the resulting count. It does not enforce an acceptance policy; the caller<br />compares the returned level with the threshold required by its application.<br /><br />Inputs:  [lookup_pow_bits, num_composed_constraints, max_constraint_degree, num_deep_terms,<br />max_message_width, num_lookup_boundary_terms, lookup_fractions_per_row,<br />log_max_height, num_queries, query_pow_bits, deep_pow_bits, folding_pow_bits, ...]<br />Outputs: [level, ...]<br /><br />Invocation: exec<br /> |
